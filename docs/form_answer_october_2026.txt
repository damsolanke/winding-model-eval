(1) Scroll data. I evaluated the released winding model, scrollprize/winding_model_9um, on PHerc0139: the 2.399 um 78 keV training scan and the 9.362 um 113 keV scan in its three reconstructions (Paganin delta/beta 1000, delta/beta 50, and no phase retrieval). I also ran it on PHerc1667, PHercMANBp and the Diamond Light Source scan of PHerc0172 (7.91 um), and tried PHerc0841, whose published segments cannot be scored (each is a single sheet with at most one winding number, so 0 of 3,000 sampled rays were usable). Labels are the published segment meshes. Everything runs through villa's own dataset, loss and metric code, unchanged, on 256 fresh rays per condition.

(2) Why it matters for reading. This model's winding numbers feed the spiral fit that unrolls a scroll, and the only metrics released for it were logged on training batches. As far as I can find, these are the first held-out numbers published for it. They show where the model can be trusted and where it cannot. On the 9.362 um scan of the same sheets it loses about 0.03 precision and 0.04 recall, and count error rises by 0.11 windings per column. With weaker phase retrieval (delta/beta 50 or none) it miscounts by 1.4 to 1.5 windings per column, so spiral-fit inputs should come from delta/beta 1000 reconstructions. On the unseen PHerc0172 scan the count error is 4.9 windings per column at native voxel size and 2.4 after resampling to the training voxel of about 9.6 um. Blurring makes things worse. I also found two duplicate meshes in PHerc0139: w042 sits on w041's sheet and w046 on w045's, 0.8 and 0.1 voxels apart, so anything that reads these segments as separate windings gets two numbers for one sheet.

(3) What it enables. Anyone can now score a winding model on new scans or reconstructions with paired comparisons on identical rays, and check a scan or a segment set before it goes into the spiral fit. It also runs on Apple Silicon. Three small villa patches (spawn-safe data workers, a mesh-unit divisor, a NumPy sampler fallback) let villa's winding-model code run on macOS with MPS, and the patched code reproduces the harness exactly on 8 slabs, label for label and voxel for voxel. A full 256-slab condition takes about 10 minutes on an M4 Max.

(4) Evidence. REPORT.md has every table with 95 % slab-bootstrap intervals, and paired intervals for each change on identical rays. Per-slab results, configs, figures and the fitted 2.399 to 9.362 um transform (0.10 voxel median residual) are in the repo. Every number reruns with the commands at the end of the report. No scroll data is redistributed.
